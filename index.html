Methodology

Every recommendation combines a transparent scoring model with statistical methods adapted from the research of Dr. Leonid Khinkis (Professor of Teaching, Institute for Artificial Intelligence and Data Science, University at Buffalo) on nonlinear regression and optimal experimental design.

01

Match score

Each program scores your results in the subjects it weighs most (60%), your interests (30%) and your career goal (10%).

02

Monte Carlo robustness

The ranking is re-run 600 times with each grade perturbed by its measurement error and program weights by ±15%, giving 90% intervals and the probability each program ranks first.

After Bucki, Khinkis et al. (2001)
03

Identifiable best fit

A recommendation is a clear best fit when it wins ≥ 80% of simulations; otherwise it is likely or a close call — analogous to a unique versus competing minima.

After Khinkis, Crotzer & Oprisan (2018)
04

D-optimal next result

The advisor finds which additional grade would most shrink the joint confidence region (det Cov) of your top-3 matches — the result worth adding next.

After Khinkis, Krzyzanski, Jusko & Greco (2009)
05

Hill readiness curves

Course readiness follows a 4-parameter Hill (sigmoid) model: it rises steeply around a threshold score EC50 and levels off near a ceiling.

After Khinkis, Levasseur, Faessel & Greco (2003)
06

AI reasoning

With AI unlocked, Gemini reviews the evidence step by step and may adjust a score by at most 8 points, explaining why. Without it, a built-in engine writes the reasoning.

Methods are adapted for an educational advising context and have not been reviewed by Dr. Khinkis. If a key subject is missing, a cautious estimate is used and flagged. With AI unlocked, results are sent to Google to generate the reasoning; otherwise everything stays in your browser.
